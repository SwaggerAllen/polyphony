Polyphony · step 8 · shared component

The audience picker

One component, answering one question: who starts out knowing this? It appears on world canon, on character facts, and on whatever carries secrets next. Getting it right once means those surfaces cost nothing.

00 · The rules it encodes

Five decisions, and why

Nobody, by default. Same default-deny as everything else. Most secrets stay empty, so the control costs nothing on the common fact and the sheet doesn't get heavier for having it.
Additive only. No exceptions. You can't say "the whole Tidewatch except Marek" — you'd name people instead. Exceptions double the mental model and the case is rare enough to pay for by hand.
Everything is an audience, including "everyone." An individual is an audience of one; common knowledge is the audience of all. So secret is really shorthand for an audience narrower than everyone — the toggle is a one-tap route to the common case, not a separate mechanism.
Two unscoped audiences do a lot of work. Everyone means common knowledge with no setting attached, and whoever was there resolves to a scene's cast. Neither needs locations, and "the whole town" is a location audience later rather than a special case now.
Groups resolve when a character enters the story, not once at t=0. This is the load-bearing one: a walk-on generated in scene 9 from the Tidewatch seed arrives already knowing. The GM never assigns anything.
An audience is always belonging, never transit. Being in a group, or being one of the people of a place — not having visited it. A main cast member who walks into the undercroft doesn't absorb its secrets; the people who work there were born into them.
Joining later doesn't backfill. If Wren joins the Tidewatch in scene 9, she doesn't silently gain the secret — she learns it in a scene. Same principle as world-arc catch-up: the reveal is fiction, not a migration.
It only sets the starting point. Everything after t=0 is play — someone told them, they were in the room. The picker's copy says starts out knowing for exactly that reason.
