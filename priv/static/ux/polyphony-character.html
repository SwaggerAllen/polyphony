Polyphony · step 9 · character sheet

The character sheet

The longest form in the product and the one people re-read most. Prose generation reuses the world editor's states and secrets reuse the audience picker, so this covers what's new: facts, relationships, the two directions a character can be pushed, groups, and the stub.

00 · Shape

A sheet is read, not just filled in

No tabs, no accordions. You come back to a sheet to remember who someone is, which means reading top to bottom. A sticky jump bar handles the length instead — position without hiding anything.
Prose first, structure after. The five written fields run continuously like a page. Facts, ties and pressures are lists, and sit below.
Nothing says "core" or "status." The model's vocabulary isn't the author's. Always in mind beats core: true and actually explains the behaviour it controls.
One boolean pattern. A small switch, right-aligned, coloured by what it means — amber for always-in-mind, violet for secret. Identical in every menu on every artifact.
Pronouns are a field, not a guess. Half the copy on this screen is written about the character — what she won't do, if she's pushed — so the interface needs them as data. It's also what the Director and every generation call should be using rather than inferring from a name.

03 · Facts

A switch and an audience, and they don't touch

They're orthogonal and shouldn't be collapsed: always in mind is whether she carries it, who else knows is who else has it. A woman can have a secret she never thinks about. Secret isn't a third thing to set — a fact is a secret once the audience names anybody, so the treatment is derived rather than stored beside an audience it could disagree with. Only one of the two can own the left border, so concealment takes the structure and always-in-mind is a chip.

The section's info drawer
About facts ×

Short, flat statements that are true about her. They're what she'd never contradict, so keep them to things you'd defend rather than things you'd like.

Always in mind

She'll have this in front of her for every turn, in every scene. Use it for the handful of things she'd never stop being aware of.

Everything else is remembered when it's relevant — she still knows it, it's just fetched rather than carried.

Who else knows

Nobody, unless you name somebody. Everyone else finds out in play, if they ever do.

There is no separate secret switch: a fact is a secret when somebody other than her is on it, so the treatment is read off the audience rather than stored beside it. The two can't fall out of step, because there is only one of them. Always-in-mind is unrelated — she can have a secret she rarely thinks about.

05 · Where she can be pushed

Two directions, and the moment each one gives

Direction lives in the grouping, not the wording. Won't and Can't stop are separate lists, so an item can never be read backwards — which is exactly what went wrong when everything was one list of "lines."
Compulsions are as dramatic as refusals. The thing she can't stop doing is the same gate with the sign flipped, and it costs nothing to support. Covering for her father is a better story engine than any refusal on the sheet.
Three lines, in sequence. The item, then until, then and then. Nobody needs a label explained when it reads as a sentence — and and then is what removes the last ambiguity about which way it flipped.
The ceiling always pushes toward refusal. A compulsion flagged for content the campaign doesn't allow is held closed, meaning she doesn't do it. That's the correct direction to fail in.

06 · Groups

Literally a character sheet, used as a seed

Same fields, same editor. A group has an appearance, a voice, a temperament, facts, relationships and pressures — because each one seeds the same-named field on anyone written from it. There is no second authoring surface and nothing new to learn.
Every field earns its place. The Tidewatch's voice seeds their members' voices. Its relationships seed theirs, so a group's contempt for the harbour office arrives in every member without being retyped.
Copied, not injected. Seeding pastes the text once, so a group costs nothing at context time and there's no group bible in any prompt. Editing the group later doesn't rewrite existing members — a seed is a starting point.
Membership is the live part. It's what secrets resolve against, it's editable by hand, and it works for characters who were never seeded from the group at all.

06b · Explaining things

One info affordance, one drawer

On the section header, never in a menu. Opening an overlay from inside an already-open menu is a mess, and it hides the explanation from anyone who didn't already go looking. The i sits beside the label.
One drawer per section, not per setting. Facts get a single drawer covering what facts are, what always in mind does and how secret works — because those three only make sense together.
It replaces inline helper text. Anywhere the current mocks explain a concept in small grey type under a control, that prose moves into the drawer and the layout gets its space back.
Same drawer everywhere. Title, prose, a subsection per concept with its own status dot. It's the sheet component already used for cast, settings and beats, so there's nothing new to build.

Quiet enough to ignore forever, obvious enough to find once.

Open · pressures
About being pushed ×

These are played, not filtered. A line she holds is a scene beat — something the story has to work against, and something that can give at the right moment.

Won't, and can't stop

Two directions. What she refuses, and what she does whether or not she means to. Both can be absolute or can turn once.

Until, and then

What has to happen before it turns, and what she's like afterwards. She isn't told the second one until it's true of her.

Flagging mature content

Only if the line is about it. A flagged item stays closed in campaigns that don't allow that content, whatever the story does.

07 · Borrowed, not redrawn

What this screen gets for free

Prose generation

Writing, proposed, replacing-your-words, failed, expand, rewrite-one-paragraph. Identical to the world bible's — drawn there.

Secrets

The audience picker, unchanged. A fact here and a canon entry in a bible are the same component with a different header.

Preview as

Author omniscient, preview read-only. Previewing her sheet as someone else hides her secrets from you the way it hides them from them.

And the group editor is this editor. Four of the five hardest things here were already built — the return on treating the picker and the prose states as components rather than page furniture.
