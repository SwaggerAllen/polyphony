Your stuff

Not a create hub any more — that's the campaign's job. This is where you find what you already have, and it's where the archive and trash finally get a front door.

00 · What changed

One create button, and characters live in campaigns

New campaign is the only create action. Worlds and characters are made inside a campaign, so the library has one button rather than three, and the three-way "what do I make first" question disappears.
Characters are grouped by campaign, not listed flat. Since characters don't cross campaigns, every one belongs to exactly one — which makes the grouping free and the list navigable even at forty walk-ons.
Library worlds are templates, not dependencies. Attaching one copies it, because a campaign writes its own history onto its world. So a row says how many campaigns started from it — past tense, no live coupling, nothing to break by editing it.
Archive and trash get a real view. Archive and delete exist in the backend with no way back; the recovery window has been a promise with no screen behind it. Restore and purge live here.

Why it's a separate shelf

A story you're reading isn't a campaign you own. You can't play it, you may not be able to fork it, and it can be unpublished out from under you.

Putting it under Campaigns would promise all three. Its own shelf promises exactly one thing — you can get back to where you were.

Needs a bookmark on the backend: scene, beat, and perspective.
05 · Visibility isn't set here

The library shows it; the editor sets it

Changing who can see something belongs next to the thing itself. You decide a world is ready to share while you're looking at the world, not while scanning a list — so private / unlisted / public lives on the world bible, the character sheet and campaign settings. The library just wears the badge.
Unlisted has never had a screen. It mints a share token today and shows it to nobody, so the link exists and is unreachable. It appears on the editor the moment you choose unlisted, with Copy as the primary action.

Worth keeping straight: this is library visibility, which governs the live artifact and its share link. Publishing a campaign is a different thing — it freezes a copy and grants a set of reading perspectives. Two systems, two homes, never merged.
